# Each school is addressed by a subdomain, with no custom domains

The site is becoming a multi-school platform: a superadmin creates schools, each school's admins manage their own content, and parents read it without an account. Every request must therefore resolve to exactly one school before anything else happens. We resolve the school from the `Host` header's leading label — `kanaka.yoursite.com` — and support no other addressing. Custom domains per school are explicitly out of scope, including for Kanaka, whose existing `kanakapac.com` becomes a permanent 301 redirect to its subdomain.

The deciding constraint was authentication, not routing. The codebase is on next-auth v4, which reads a single `NEXTAUTH_URL` (`src/lib/auth-options.ts`). Custom domains make every school a separate browser origin with its own cookie jar, which would force a next-auth v5 migration (or a hand-rolled session layer) before the second school could be onboarded. Subdomains keep the whole platform on one origin family, so a session cookie scoped to `.yoursite.com` covers every school with one login. The secondary constraint was operations: a subdomain school is provisioned by inserting a row, while a custom-domain school requires the school to own a domain, a PAC volunteer to edit DNS records, and a wait on certificate issuance — per school, forever, with a solo maintainer as the support desk.

The apex domain serves a marketing page and the superadmin login. An unrecognized subdomain returns a 404 linking back to the apex. A suspended school is treated differently: it serves a plain notice saying the site is temporarily unavailable, which does confirm that the slug exists. That disclosure is accepted deliberately — school slugs are school names and effectively guessable regardless — because a parent meeting a bare 404 on a site that worked last week is a real support problem, and an accurate notice is worth more than concealing a fact that is not secret.

## Considered Options

- **Subdomain only** (chosen): one origin family, one wildcard certificate, one verified email sending domain, instant provisioning. Costs Kanaka its established domain and makes every school visibly hosted on a shared platform.
- **Custom domain only**: strongest per-school identity and credibility with PAC executives. Rejected because it forces a next-auth v5 migration before school #2, requires per-school email domain verification, puts DNS configuration in the hands of volunteers, and makes a lapsed registration someone else's outage that we get blamed for.
- **Subdomain always, with an optional custom-domain alias**: a permanent subdomain for auth and email, with a custom domain serving public content only. Keeps the auth model simple while preserving per-school identity. Rejected as unnecessary complexity for the current school count — two canonical URLs per school means a canonical-URL rule for emails and SEO, plus the custom-domain resolution machinery this decision exists to avoid.
- **Path prefix** (`yoursite.com/kanaka/...`): no DNS or certificate work at all and works in local dev immediately. Rejected because all schools would share one cookie jar, making session isolation purely an application concern with no help from the browser.

## Consequences

- Kanaka's users experience a visible URL change. `kanakapac.com` must be renewed indefinitely as a redirect, or its remaining inbound links — including printed newsletters and district directory listings — break.
- Wildcard DNS and wildcard TLS are available on every Vercel plan including the free Hobby tier, confirmed against Vercel's multi-tenant limits documentation on 2026-10-15. This was the prerequisite that could have reopened this decision; it does not. The remaining requirement is a configuration one: Vercel must be able to answer the DNS challenge to issue and renew the wildcard certificate, so the apex either uses Vercel's nameservers or delegates `_acme-challenge` to Vercel.
- Hobby caps custom domains at fifty per project, but under this decision every school is served by the single wildcard rather than being a custom domain, so the cap does not apply. Under the rejected custom-domain-only option it would have become a hard ceiling at fifty schools.
- Local development and the Playwright suite can no longer assume a single host. Dev uses `<slug>.localhost`, and `e2etest/playwright.config.ts`'s single `PLAYWRIGHT_BASE_URL` is replaced by a per-school URL helper. Safari does not resolve `*.localhost`.
- Because schools cannot present their own domain, per-school identity rests entirely on in-app branding — school name, logo, accent colour, and contact details stored per school in `settings`.
- Adding custom domains later is possible but is a genuine reopening of this decision, not an extension: it reintroduces the multi-origin session problem that subdomain-only was chosen to avoid.
